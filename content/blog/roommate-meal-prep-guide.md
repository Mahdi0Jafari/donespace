Sharing a kitchen with roommates can either be a fun, social experience or a passive-aggressive nightmare involving stolen milk and overflowing trash cans.

When it comes to groceries and meal prep, ambiguity is your enemy. "I'll buy it this time, you get it next time" usually ends with one person feeling financially strained. 

Here is a survival guide for setting up equitable meal systems with your roommates.

## Step 1: Define Your "Sharing Level"

Before you buy a single carton of eggs, sit down and decide on your household's sharing model. 

1. **Every Person for Themselves:** You buy and prepare your own food entirely. This is ideal for roommates with drastically different budgets, schedules, or dietary restrictions (e.g., one vegan, one carnivore). Keep a small "shared" shelf for absolute basics like salt, pepper, and cooking oil.
2. **Staples Only:** You buy your own personal meals, but split the cost of frequently used staples like milk, butter, coffee, and toilet paper.
3. **Full Communal:** You pool funds to buy all groceries together and share most meals. This is highly cost-effective but requires deep trust and alignment on food preferences.

## Step 2: Establish Equitable Systems

If you choose to share anything (even just staples), you need a system to keep the math fair.

- **Expense-Splitting Apps:** Use tools like Splitwise or Mr.Splitter. If you buy the shared coffee beans, immediately log it. This removes the awkwardness of "chasing" your roommate for $5.
- **Shared Shopping Lists:** A piece of paper on the fridge is easily lost. Use a real-time shared digital list so whoever is at the store knows exactly what the house needs.
- **Rotate Categories:** If you do communal dinners, don't just split costs—split the labor. If Roommate A cooks on Tuesday, Roommate B cleans the kitchen. 

## Step 3: Kitchen Etiquette & Boundaries

Even in a highly communal house, boundaries are essential.
- **Labeling:** If you bought an expensive jar of pesto for a specific recipe, label it with your name.
- **The Golden Rule:** If you are not sure if an item is shared, *ask first*. If you didn't buy it, don't eat it.
- **Coordinate Schedules:** If your kitchen is small, create a rough cooking schedule so you aren't fighting for stove space at 6:30 PM.
